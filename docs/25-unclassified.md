# 25 - Miscellaneous root and small files

[中文版](25-unclassified.zh-CN.md)

## Summary

The unclassified group has 13 rows from two partitions: 12 symbolic links (11 in system_a, 1 in vendor_a) and one regular file, system/xbin/su. The links are absolute-path aliases to /data/cache, /system/bin, /system/etc, /storage/self/primary, /sys/kernel/debug, /odm/odm_dlkm/etc, /vendor/vendor_dlkm/etc, a shell bugreports directory under /data, and the /system_ext, /vendor, /product and /odm partition roots. plat_file_contexts labels these paths as rootfs, cache_file, vendor_file or system_file. The su file is a stripped AArch64 PIE ELF with interpreter /system/bin/linker64. It needs libc.so, libc++.so, libm.so and libdl.so, and the libc, libm and libdl names resolve through symlinks into the com.android.runtime APEX. Its usage text is "su [WHO [COMMAND...]]" with default shell /system/bin/sh, its mode bits are 4750 with owner 0:2000, and its file_contexts label is su_exec. The policy declares su and su_exec and contains a shell-to-su process transition. The system build.prop in the image reports Android release 12 and build type userdebug.

Role table: `data/subsystems/unclassified/roles.tsv` (13 files: 13 verified, 0 observed, 0 inferred, 0 unverified).

## Verified claims

- **U01** (verified): The group holds 13 rows: 12 symbolic links and 1 regular file. Split by partition: system_a has 11 links and 1 file, vendor_a has 1 link.
- **U02** (verified): The file row system_a/system/xbin/su has size 11088 bytes and sha256 9ec7f36532bfbc20bd9702897a10cba7f8d78b54b24a64d836e6d9d2db3fafb6, equal to the value recorded in classified.tsv.
- **U03** (verified): su is a stripped 64-bit little-endian AArch64 ELF of type DYN (PIE), with 11 program headers, 25 section headers and entry point 0x1000. Its interpreter is /system/bin/linker64 and its build ID is 0a22c7e5ae892af0d48d6a83b14ea2fd.
- **U05** (verified): The dynamic section lists four NEEDED libraries (libc++.so, libc.so, libm.so, libdl.so), flags BIND_NOW and NOW PIE, and no RPATH or RUNPATH entry.
- **U06** (verified): su imports 22 undefined dynamic symbols, all versioned against LIBC (version 2) from libc.so: __libc_init, __errno, __stack_chk_fail, error, execvp, free, fwrite, getpwnam, getpwuid, getuid, memset, setenv, setgid, setgroups, setuid, stderr (OBJECT), strcmp, strcpy, strdup, strsep, strtoul, unsetenv.
- **U07** (verified): The ELF notes contain an Android NT_VERSION note whose description data is 20 00 00 00 and a GNU build-id note with value 0a22c7e5ae892af0d48d6a83b14ea2fd.
- **U08** (verified): In the extracted tree su has mode bits 4750 (-rwsr-x---) with owner 0 and group 2000, and its parent directory xbin has mode 750 with owner 0:2000.
- **U09** (verified): The binary embeds the usage text 'usage: su [WHO [COMMAND...]]', a description that WHO defaults to 'root' and COMMAND defaults to sh, and a note that WHO is a comma-separated list of user, group and supplementary groups in that order.
- **U11** (verified): The .gnu_debugdata section is an xz stream that decompresses to a 64-bit AArch64 ELF object with five FUNC symbols: _start (20 bytes), _start_main (64), main (672), pwtoid (220) and extract_uidgids (592). Demangled, the two helpers are pwtoid(char const*, unsigned int*, unsigned int*) and extract_uidgids(char const*, unsigned int*, unsigned int*, unsigned int*, int*).
- **U12** (verified): plat_file_contexts maps /system/xbin/su to the label u:object_r:su_exec:s0. No other line in the file mentions xbin.
- **U13** (verified): plat_sepolicy.cil declares type su and type su_exec, each with an object_r role. su is declared typepermissive.
- **U14** (verified): su_exec is a member of the attribute sets file_type (line 738), exec_type (line 740) and system_file_type (line 751) in plat_sepolicy.cil.
- **U15** (verified): plat_sepolicy.cil contains policy statements naming su or su_exec: a neverallow on base_typeattr_283 for execute and execute_no_trans on su_exec, allow rules from shell and su to su_exec for file execute and read, and a typetransition from shell executing su_exec to process su.
- **U17** (verified): The dynamic-linker and bionic dependencies of su resolve through symlinks. linker64 points to /apex/com.android.runtime/bin/linker64, and libc.so, libm.so and libdl.so point to /apex/com.android.runtime/lib64/bionic/. libc++.so is present in system/lib64 as a file of 686024 bytes, and /system/bin/sh is present as a file of 316024 bytes.
- **U19** (verified): No .rc file in the mounted tree contains the string xbin, so no init script references the /system/xbin directory or su.
- **U21** (verified): Four further links point to partition roots: system/system_ext to /system_ext, system/vendor to /vendor, system/product to /product (all in system_a), and odm in vendor_a to /odm.
- **U22** (verified): Every link in the group is mode 644 (lrw-r--r--), owned by root:root, and its st_size equals the length of its target string (for example 11 for /data/cache, 21 for /storage/self/primary, 50 for the bugreports path).
- **U27** (inferred): The 12 links are most likely static content of the partition images, created when the images were built, rather than links made by init at boot. This is inferred from the absence of any init statement that creates them and from the identical 2009-01-01 timestamps on all entries.
- **U28** (verified): The system build.prop in the image reports ro.build.version.release=12 and ro.build.type=userdebug.
- **U29** (inferred): The su binary is a switch-user command. From its strings and imports it takes an optional WHO and COMMAND, sets the group and user IDs and the supplementary group list, and executes COMMAND or /system/bin/sh. The helper names suggest that one helper parses the WHO string into IDs and another extracts UID, GID and group list.

## Claims with corrected wording

- **U04** (verified): su has 11 program headers: PHDR, INTERP, three LOAD segments (R, R+E, RW; alignment 0x1000), TLS, DYNAMIC, GNU_RELRO, GNU_EH_FRAME, GNU_STACK (RW) and NOTE. A non-allocated .gnu_debugdata section sits at file offset 0x23de with size 0x130.
- **U10** (verified): The binary embeds --help, setuid failed, setgid failed, invalid uid/gid '%s', setgroups failed, not allowed, too many group ids and failed to exec %s, plus the environment names PATH, USER, LOGNAME, IFS. The default shell path /system/bin/sh and a default PATH literal (/product/bin:/apex/com.android.runtime/bin:...:/vendor/xbin) are embedded. The binary passes PATH, USER and LOGNAME to setenv (PATH with the default literal as value), and clears IFS, USER and LOGNAME with unsetenv at separate call sites. It imports no getenv, so these names are set or cleared rather than read.
- **U16** (verified): su_exec also appears in the vendor and system_ext policy files and in the versioned mapping files. The platform public versioned policy file stored on vendor_a (plat_pub_versioned.cil, lines 4253-4255) redeclares it as su_exec_32_0, and the compiled odm precompiled_sepolicy contains the string.
- **U18** (verified): The APEX archive com.android.runtime.apex is a zip (JAR) of 8955305 bytes whose payload is apex_payload.img (8880128 bytes). It also holds apex_manifest.pb, apex_pubkey, apex_build_info.pb, resources.arsc, AndroidManifest.xml, assets/NOTICE.html.gz and META-INF (CERT.SF, CERT.RSA, MANIFEST.MF), 10 entries in all. The payload was not unpacked in this group.
- **U20** (verified): Eight links in system_a point to absolute targets: six at the root (cache -> /data/cache, bin -> /system/bin, sdcard -> /storage/self/primary, d -> /sys/kernel/debug, etc -> /system/etc, bugreports -> /data/user_de/0/com.android.shell/files/bugreports), and two one level down inside the directories vendor_dlkm/ and odm_dlkm/ (vendor_dlkm/etc -> /vendor/vendor_dlkm/etc, odm_dlkm/etc -> /odm/odm_dlkm/etc). The system_a root also holds init -> /system/bin/init, which is not in this group.
- **U23** (verified): The link targets /vendor/vendor_dlkm/etc and /odm/odm_dlkm/etc are absent under vendor_a and odm_a. The etc directories exist as real directories only in vendor_dlkm_a and odm_dlkm_a. The directories vendor_dlkm and odm_dlkm also exist at the root of system_a (each holding only the etc link) and in the overlay directory of the scratch partition (without etc).
- **U24** (verified): plat_file_contexts labels the link paths. Partition-root links are matched by pattern rules on lines 1 (vendor), 23 (odm), 43 (product), 55 (system_ext), 70 (vendor_dlkm) and 71 (odm_dlkm). Pattern rules /cache(/.*)? on line 86 and /data/cache(/.*)? on line 125 are labelled cache_file. Plain path rules for /d, /bin, /etc, /sdcard and /bugreports on lines 351, 354, 355, 362 and 384 are labelled rootfs.
- **U25** (verified): Init and property scripts reference the link paths or their targets. /cache is used by chown, chmod, restorecon_recursive and mkdir at init.rc lines 580-587. /bin/rm is used at init.rc line 1203. /sdcard is exported as EXTERNAL_STORAGE in init.environ.rc line 11. /d/mmc0 is declared with read mode in the storaged service (storaged.rc line 5). /system/vendor/bin/cnss-daemon is defined as a service in class late_start at vendor init.anorak.rc line 227. The bugreports target path is used by the bugreport service at vendor init.anorak.rc line 210.
- **U26** (verified): The symlink statements in init.rc create none of the group's link names. They create /dev/net/tun, /dev/stdin, /dev/stdout, /dev/stderr, /dev/binder, /dev/hwbinder, /dev/vndbinder, /mnt/sdcard, /mnt/runtime/default/self/primary, /dev/fd, /dev/sys/fs/by-name/userdata and /dev/sys/block/by-name/userdata.
- **U30** (inferred): Each of the 12 links is a plain alias for its target path. Its role is limited to the target path verified in U20 and U21 and the arrival of the target in other partitions. Init scripts reference cache, sdcard, bin, d and system/vendor through their link paths (see U25), so those purposes have observed path references. The purposes of the bugreports, dlkm, etc, product, system_ext and odm links have no consumer confirmed in the files checked.

## Gaps

- The payload apex_payload.img inside com.android.runtime.apex was not unpacked. The contents of linker64, libc.so, libm.so and libdl.so, and their symbol versions, were not examined because they belong to another subsystem.
- The su machine code was not disassembled: the available objdump reported "can't disassemble for architecture UNKNOWN!" for this AArch64 file. Control flow, the exact system calls and the run-time behaviour of su were not established, and su was not executed.
- The embedded debug data holds five FUNC symbols only, with no DWARF or type information. Local functions, data objects and string-to-code cross-references are unresolved.
- Extended attributes and SELinux labels on the symlink inodes and on the su inode were not read. The labels given here come from file_contexts patterns only, and the effective label of each path was not confirmed.
- The runtime targets /data/cache, /data/user_de/0/com.android.shell/files/bugreports, /storage/self/primary and /sys/kernel/debug are outside the mounted set, so their existence and contents were not verified.
- The vendor_dlkm/etc and odm_dlkm/etc targets are not present under the vendor_a or odm_a trees. Their resolution through the separate dlkm partitions is inferred from the partition names and was not verified against an fstab.
- The device mount points for system_a, vendor_a, odm_a, product_a and system_ext_a were inferred from the link targets. The fstab or init mount entries were not read.
- The compiled policy odm_a/etc/selinux/precompiled_sepolicy was checked only for the presence of the string su_exec. It was not decoded, and effective access rules for su and su_exec were not computed.
- Only the policy statements that name su or su_exec were listed. The full rule set for the su domain and the su_exec type was not enumerated.
- The build step or manifest that created the 12 links was not located, so the origin of the links is inferred only.
- The mode and ownership values come from the extracted tree (stat on the mounted copy), not from the original image's inode metadata.
- The roles given for the symlinks describe their targets. Their purposes (aliases, legacy paths, compatibility) are inferred from names and were not confirmed by a consumer of each link.
